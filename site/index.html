MMA Career Rankings

Rank fighters by career (All-time), best ten years (Prime), or recent form (Current). One model rates every recorded professional MMA fight, from every promotion. All-time and Prime rank careers from the UFC and the other major promotions, so a fighter can rank without a UFC fight. Current and weight-class boards use UFC bouts.

How to read the scores

How All-time is calculated

The score combines championships (30%), career skill (17.5%) and contender wins (52.5%). Title and contender wins are weighted by opponent quality. Career skill measures active years above the division's contender level.

Each part is divided by the average of its own 100 highest positive values, then square-rooted and weighted. The total is multiplied by 1,000. The square root limits how much one extreme part can dominate. These weights are chosen scoring rules. A score is a points total, not a percentage advantage over another fighter.

Why a rating can fall after a win

The career chart shows estimated skill at each bout, not accumulated points. The model fits whole careers, so later results can revise earlier estimates.

The chart also adjusts each year against that year's contender baseline. If the baseline rises faster than a fighter's raw rating, the plotted line falls even after a win. This happens in GSP's career. A downward step does not necessarily mean the win hurt him.

The top-100 normalization applies to the All-time score, not this line. Between releases, a retired fighter's score can fall as the comparison pool improves or the model revises past ratings.

The math, in plain terms

Whole-History Rating

A chess-style rating fitted to every career at once (Coulom, 2008). The gap between two ratings gives each fighter's chance of winning. Because nothing is fixed one fight at a time, a win counts for more when the opponent later proves to be great, and fighters from different decades share one scale.

A neutral starting bout

Each fighter starts with the weight of one imaginary fight against an average opponent, half won and half lost. Without it, a short unbeaten record has no upper limit and could top the board.

Graded results

A finish counts as a full win; decisions and disqualifications count slightly less, and a draw counts as half. A knockout says more about who was better than a split decision does.

A contender line for each year

Every rating is read against its division's fifth-best fighter that year. The rating scale drifts upward over time, so this compares a 2007 win and a 2023 win fairly.

Win probability raised to a power

A win is valued by the chance the beaten opponent would have beaten a contender: to the fourth power for a title win, the third power for other contender wins. One elite win is worth about five borderline ones, without letting a single win carry a career. The third power was chosen after testing powers 1 to 6.

Promotion weights, pulled toward average

The rating never looks at the promotion. Afterwards, skill and contender-win points are scaled by where they were earned, between 0.20 and 1.00 (UFC 1.00). A career with few promotion-labelled fights is pulled toward the average, so a handful of fights cannot decide it.

Square roots on a common scale

Titles, contender wins and skill are each measured against their top-100 average, square-rooted, then weighted. A career strong in all three beats one huge number in a single part.

Best ten-year window

Prime finds the ten-year stretch with the most wins over contenders and needs at least four. Each win beyond that adds to the score. A ten-year window keeps a peak from being diluted by a long career before or after it.

The model has not yet been shown to predict fights better than a simpler rating. These are measured judgements of careers, not betting odds.

Fighter comparisons

Estimated skill at each bout, up to six fighters: each year is shifted by how far its contender line sat from the average year's, so careers in different eras share one scale. Later bouts revise earlier ratings. This is a hindsight estimate, not a running points total; it can fall after a win.

Career scores by year

Career mass adds, for each active year, how far the fighter's average rating stood above that year's bar; no year subtracts.
