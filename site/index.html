Every section

Three boards from one fit of every recorded MMA career.

One rating model is fitted to every recorded bout at once, so a win counts for more the better the opponent proved to be. Three questions are then asked of it: what a career was worth (All-time), how good a fighter was across their best ten years (Prime) and how good they are now (Current). Each section below makes one claim and shows the evidence. The controls in the bar above apply to every section.

The three boards ask different questions, so their top tens differ.

These are the published boards, filtered by the bar above. Choosing a weight class on the All-time board shows that class's own board: the All-time score counted only from what was earned in that class. A fighter's class scores add up exactly to their All-time score, so a two-division champion is ranked in both. Prime and Current within a class keep the published order, renumbered. A blank Prime rank means the fighter did not qualify; it does not mean they placed last. Select a name to add it to Fighters.

The All-time score is a weighted sum of three parts.

All-time = 1000 × (a·championships + (1 − a)·(s·career skill + (1 − s)·contender résumé)). Each part is divided by the mean of its own top 100 before weighting. The bars add up exactly to the published total. Move the two exchange rates to re-rank the same eligible fighters; who is eligible does not depend on them.

Long records and high scores go together.

Each dot is a fighter on the board chosen under The rankings. Across is the number of rated bouts; up is that board's figure. The ten leaders are marked.

A rating arc shows when a career peaked and how long it held there.

Base WHR skill after each rated bout, for up to six fighters. The cards compare what each career did; they do not predict a bout between them.

Career mass is years above the bar times the height above it.

Career skill mass adds up, over a fighter's active years, how far their average rating that year stood above the year's bar. The bar is the contender line of their division, or the sport-wide line where the division is too thin. A year below the bar adds almost nothing, and no year subtracts.

A single year can move a rating by hundreds of points.

The largest rating changes of one year among fighters eligible for the boards. Hover over a bar to see the bouts behind the move.

The longest UFC win streaks.

Unbroken UFC win runs. A draw or a loss ends a run; a no-contest neither extends nor ends it. Average opponent is the opponents' Glicko-2 rating today. Select a row to see that fighter's rating timeline.

Weight classes differ in strength, and the strongest class changes over time.

The class chips and years here apply only to this section. The board-share chart follows the board chosen under The rankings.

Title reigns differ in length by an order of magnitude.

Each class's belt drawn as one track. Each segment is a reign, from winning the title to the next title win by someone else.

Some fighters win far more one-sidedly than others.

Mean dominance per win: strike differential, control time and submission attempts, combined. It is not an input to any rating.

The integrity policy is a stated judgement, kept apart from the boards.

Fixed debits to base WHR points for wins with a confirmed PED violation, a disqualification or a missed weight. The policy never changes a published board, and it is not presented as an improvement in prediction.

The pre-fight forecasts are close to calibrated.

Before each bout, the Glicko-2 ratings give each fighter a probability of winning. Bouts are grouped by that probability, and each group's forecast is compared with how often those fighters actually won. Only past bouts enter a pre-fight rating, so this tests forecasts made before the result was known. Both fighters must have at least three earlier rated bouts, and draws are excluded.

The All-time board broadly agrees with FightMatrix's all-time list.

The men's All-time board against FightMatrix's all-time list. This is a check, not a target. FightMatrix measures whole careers under its own definition, so a disagreement names a fighter worth a closer look; it is not an error by itself.
